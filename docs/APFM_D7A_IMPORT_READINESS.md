# APFM D7A — Import readiness: can the whole FM24 database become a playable world?

**Status:** assessment only. **Date:** 2026-11-11. **Branch:** `deepseek/d7-database-audit`.
**Companion:** [`APFM_D7A_SOURCE_DISCOVERY.md`](APFM_D7A_SOURCE_DISCOVERY.md) — paths, hashes,
schema, counts and mapping evidence.

---

## 1. Decision

**NO-GO for importing the entire source database as a fully simulated world in its current
form.**

**GO for a staged, opt-in D7B expansion**, one league pyramid at a time, once the blockers in §3
are closed. Nothing in D7B may bundle FM data or change M12 / the match engine / the frontend
unless a phase explicitly says so.

The rest of this document explains what “the whole database” means, what is actually missing,
which parts of the architecture already support expansion, and what a safe sequence looks like.

### 1.1 Decision by scope

| Scope | What it means | Size | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| **L0 — shipped world** | 12 leagues / 6 cups, 240 clubs | 13,157 players | **GO (already shipped)** | Validated by `apfm-import` (23/23 checks) and the season harness |
| **L1 — same six nations, full pyramids** | Add League Two, Serie C, Primera Federación and reserve/B sides | ~300–360 clubs | **GO, conditional** | Same rules/reference data family; needs target rows, rules and constants made configurable |
| **L2 — all 99 leagues with 2026 membership** | Multi-nation active pyramid | 1,184 clubs, ~31,786 contracted players | **NO-GO now; GO after B1–B3, B13** | 86 leagues have no target/level/rules; ~48× season cost |
| **L3 — all clubs / all players** | Whole save as one simulated world | 44,032 clubs, 81,762 players | **NO-GO** | ~10^5× season cost; no plausible product need |
| **L4 — dormant-only wide world** | All clubs imported but resolved by scoreline | 44,032 clubs | **NO-GO now; research later** | Still needs calendars, memberships, finances and a thin team model; unproven |

“L2/L3” are the only readings of “import the whole database”. Neither is ready.

| Clubs | 44,032 | 240 | 0.55 % |

| Competitions | 1,585 | 12 leagues + 6 cups | 1.1 % |
| Contract clauses | 190,321 | 0 | 0 % |
| Fixtures | 28,888 | generated by OFM | — |

The shipped world is not a small sample of a uniform database: it is one **curated perimeter**
(12 leagues in 6 nations) with a bounded free-agent pool. Widening it is a modelling project, not
a re-run with different filters.

Note on revisions: the world installed on this machine is revision 4
(`fm24-install.json`), while the current adapter is `WORLD_REVISION = 5`
(`src-tauri/crates/fm24-adapter/src/install.rs:18`). The counts above are the same in the
`output/m7world` revision-5 reference build and in the installed revision-4 file.

## 3. Blockers

Each blocker states the evidence, why it blocks, and what would close it. “Blocking” means it
prevents L2/L3; L1 is mainly blocked by B7, B13 and the missing rules for the three divisions it
would add.

### B1 — Simulation cost does not scale

**Evidence.** `src-tauri/crates/ofm_core/src/world/foundations.rs:32` documents that “a simulated
season costs about n^2.43 in the size of the world”. The same comment (line 1016) sizes test
worlds around that cost. The documented 5-season full-world harness run takes ~70 s for 240
clubs (`docs/APFM_MARKET_FINAL_VALIDATION.md`).

**Impact.** Applying the documented exponent as an order-of-magnitude estimate:

| Clubs | Ratio vs 240 | Estimated season cost | 5-season harness estimate |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 240 | 1× | 1× | ~70 s |
| 360 (L1) | 1.5× | 2.7× | ~3 min |
| 1,184 (L2) | 4.9× | ~48× | ~56 min |
| 2,000 | 8.3× | ~173× | ~3.4 h |
| 44,032 (L3) | 183× | ~3×10^5× | months/years |

These are estimates from the code's own exponent, not benchmarks. They are enough to rule out L3
as a daily-play product and to make L2 a deliberate performance project.

**Closes when.** A measured benchmark harness exists for 360 / 1,184 clubs, a per-season budget is
agreed, and the answer for L2 is either “fits the budget with dormant tiers” or “cap the active
pyramid and keep the rest dormant”.

### B2 — 86 of 99 leagues have no modelling data

**Evidence.** 99 `league` competitions have 2026 membership; **86** have no `competition_target`
row and **87** lack a name, nation or pyramid level. Only 12 are `playable`/`simulated`. The
adapter's `competitions.rs::build` requires a nation and a pyramid level for each competition
(and uses the target key to find authored rules), and fails otherwise.

**Impact.** Importing a league is not automatic: each needs a target row (key, name, nation,
level, scope, promotion/relegation links), sporting rules (`rules.rs`), and calendar/season
metadata. The reference data under `fm24-adapter/config` and `refs.rs` was hand-authored for the
12 leagues; it does not cover the other 87.

**Closes when.** A reference-data pipeline (or an explicit, reviewed data set) exists for each
league added, with provenance, and `checks.rs` validates coverage instead of hard-coding 12.

### B3 — Competition structure is only partially modelled

**Evidence.** Source kinds: 99 leagues, 6 cups, 3 continental, 1,477 `other`. The adapter builds
12 leagues and 6 national cups; continental cups are deliberately excluded (“no verified European
rules”, `competitions.rs:1-8`). 1,477 `other` competitions (youth, reserves, regional, friendly
and historical) are ignored. `competition_season_team` has 2,879 rows across all kinds.

**Impact.** A “whole database” world would either (a) simulate a competition graph the adapter
cannot describe, or (b) silently drop most competitions, which contradicts the goal. European
qualification and continental revenue also do not exist in the current model.

**Closes when.** The product scope explicitly says which competition classes are in, and a
definition exists for each class. Continental competitions need their own rules and calendar
before they can be claimed.

### B4 — Player volume exceeds what daily systems are designed for

**Evidence.** 81,762 players; 20,448 with no current contract; 61,314 contracted. The shipped
world imports 13,157 and caps free agents at 3,000 of 10,480 eligible
(`free_agents.rs`, `config/adapter.toml`). `free_agents.rs` states that putting all unattached
players in would “triple the number of players every daily scan walks and the state sent to the
screen”.

**Impact.** Daily transfer/contract/AI scans, the save payload and the UI all grow with player
count. The installed world JSON is 21 MB for 13,157 players; a linear L2/L3 estimate is
~50 MB / ~130 MB before compression, and the package format loads it as one document.

**Closes when.** There is a measured budget for daily scans and payload size, and the free-agent
and inactive-player model is defined (pool vs full population).

### B5 — Source data quality is uneven

**Evidence.** 145 players have no name and no birth date (144 unattached); 698 players have no
nation; 42 clubs have no nation; 34 clubs have no reputation; 3,209 players have no contract end;
3,533 players have zero wages in every current contract; 9,718 current contracts have wage 0.

**Impact.** The adapter already has fallbacks (nation fallback, configured contract end, generated
wages/values), but each fallback is a product decision that must hold at 4–10× the current volume.
Clubs without a nation cannot be placed in a national pyramid without a decision.

**Closes when.** Every fallback is documented, bounded and counted in the import report, and the
new leagues' data is checked for the same classes of gap before import.

### B6 — Contracts and clauses are mostly not modelled

**Evidence.** 190,321 contract clauses in source; OFM models wage, start, end, club, team and
identity only. `contracts.py` builds chains, but the adapter does not map clause kinds, release
fees, sell-on percentages, bonuses or option years.

**Impact.** Expanding player volume multiplies an already lossy contract model. Wage-0 contracts
and missing ends become more visible, and transfer/finance realism depends on values the source
does not carry in the mapped fields.

**Closes when.** Either clauses are explicitly out of scope (documented as a known limitation) or
a clause model and migration exist. For L2 this must be a written decision, not an omission.

### B7 — Hard-coded world shape

**Evidence.** `src-tauri/crates/fm24-adapter/src/pipeline.rs:112-115`:

```rust
pub const EXPECTED_CLUBS: usize = 240;
pub const EXPECTED_COMPETITIONS: usize = 12;
pub const EXPECTED_CUPS: usize = 6;
```

`pipeline.rs::run` rejects a club that appears in two perimeter competitions; `checks.rs` fails on
any other shape. `perimeter()` filters `kind='league' AND (playable=1 OR simulated=1)`.

**Impact.** L1 already breaks the constants. Any expansion must make the expected shape derive
from the selected target set and update checks, report and reference data together.

**Closes when.** Expected counts come from the target configuration, with checks that assert
coverage and uniqueness rather than a fixed 240/12/6. This is the first code change of D7B.

### B8 — Identity and duplicate FM UIDs

**Evidence.** 165 FM UIDs are shared by 342 persons; 26 UIDs span multiple players; club and
competition UIDs are unique. The adapter keys on internal ids (`fm-p-<person.id>`), and the
reference run keeps 17 shared-UID players distinct (`ids.shared_fm_uid_kept_distinct`).

**Impact.** At the current scale the adapter handles it, but any feature that joins on FM UID
(scouting, external data, photos) must not assume uniqueness. Expanding clubs also expands the
chance of duplicate or unstable membership rows.

**Closes when.** All joins use internal ids, the shared-UID rule is tested on the expanded set,
and the import report counts shared UIDs per league.

### B9 — Staff model covers four roles

**Evidence.** `staff.rs::map_role` maps head coach → manager, and assistant / coach / scout /
physio; everything else is `Excluded` (`staff.role_not_imported`, 14 occurrences). Source has
45,430 staff, 17,604 with no current contract; the world imports 4,116 + 141 generated.

**Impact.** A wider world will import many more staff but still drop directors, analysts, owners
and other roles. Squad/staff floors, wages and finances must stay coherent when most staff of a
club are excluded.

**Closes when.** The excluded-role policy is documented per league and the staff floor is
validated at the target scale.

### B10 — Scope gating is real but incomplete

**Evidence.** `Game::competition_in_active_scope` (`game.rs:470`) and `active_team_ids`
(`game.rs:487`) gate the turn/day loop (`turn/mod.rs:48`), squad selection (`turn/squad.rs:523`),
training (`training.rs:186,216`), the transfer market (`transfers/market.rs:468`), assistant and
delegation (`staff_system/assistant.rs:114`, `staff_system/delegation.rs:189`). Dormant
competitions are resolved by scoreline (`turn/dormant.rs`). Seasonal aging and youth intake run
over every club (`end_of_season/mod.rs:886,888`) and do not consult the active scope; that is
intentional but is an O(all players) annual cost.

**Impact.** The architecture already has the right shape for a wide, mostly dormant world. What is
missing is proof that the dormant tier is complete for the new competitions (calendar, standings,
promotion/relegation, cup progression, finances) and that the annual global passes stay within
budget at 10× players.

**Closes when.** Dormant-tier coverage tests exist for every imported competition class and the
annual passes are benchmarked at the target size.

### B11 — Licensing and distribution

**Evidence.** `NOTICE-APFM.md`, `.gitignore` lines 61–64, and
`src-tauri/tests/distribution_guard.rs` forbid bundling `world.db`, built FM24 worlds or derived
data. The source is proprietary SI/SEGA data.

**Impact.** D7B can only ship code and local import. No prebuilt expanded world, no sample
database, no CI fixture built from real FM data. Tests must use synthetic fixtures.

**Closes when.** Every D7B phase keeps the guard tests green and adds no FM-derived fixture to
git or the bundle. This is a permanent constraint, not a one-time blocker.

### B12 — Save-format and version coupling

**Evidence.** The save is FMF (`fmsave` container), FM `24.3.0`, importer `1.1.0`, schema `v3`.
The importer lives outside this repository and vendors a save reader. The adapter validates
`schema_facts` and the source hash.

**Impact.** A future FM build or a different save can change sections, attribute layouts or
membership encoding. Expansion increases the surface that must be re-validated. There is no
guarantee the 2026/27 update's league membership is stable across releases.

**Closes when.** Version detection, schema facts and a clear failure path are tested, and the
supported source versions are documented.

### B13 — Financial and calendar realism widen with the pyramid

**Evidence.** The current build logs 29 `finance.wage_budget_incompatible` and 71
`finance.wage_budget_raised_to_commitments` warnings. OFM income is a flat gate plus a configured
sponsorship because FM24 wage bills are far above OFM's native income
(`config/adapter.toml`, `[finance]`). Promotion/relegation, TV money and cup revenue exist only
for the modelled leagues.

**Impact.** Adding lower divisions adds clubs with small wage bills and small income; the
configured sponsorship and budget rules were calibrated on the current 240 clubs. L1/L2 need a
financial calibration pass per division, or clubs will be insolvent or artificially rich.

**Closes when.** A per-division finance calibration exists and multi-season runs show stable
budgets, transfers and wage bills for the added divisions.

### B14 — UI and save payload at scale

**Evidence.** The installed world is 21 MB / 13,157 players. `install_fm24_world` runs the build
on a blocking thread and notes it “takes a couple of seconds and holds a few hundred MB”. The
package is loaded as one document.

**Impact.** L2/L3 multiply file size, parse time, memory and the amount of state the frontend
receives. No current screen is designed for 1,000+ clubs or 80,000 players.

**Closes when.** Payload size and load time are measured, lazy loading or projection is designed
where needed, and no screen regresses.

## 4. What already supports expansion

This is not a greenfield project. The following are assets, not blockers:

* **A read-only adapter with determinism.** `apfm-import` hashes the source before and after,
  builds from a seeded config (`seed = 20262027`), and writes a reproducible report. The reference
  run passes 23/23 checks, including `source.untouched` and `ids.internal_keys_only`.
* **A simulation-scope model.** `active_competition_ids` / `active_region_ids`, empty meaning
  “everything”, are consumed by the expensive daily subsystems (B10). A wide world can be mostly
  dormant without changing the match engine.
* **A dormant tier.** `turn/dormant.rs` resolves out-of-scope fixtures by scoreline, updates
  standings and advances group/knockout progression without per-player work.
* **A generic competition builder.** `world/build.rs` and
  `build_foundation_competitions` already build pyramids from teams and a division size, used by
  the season harness. The adapter's `competitions.rs` emits OFM `CompetitionDefinition`s.
* **Placement rules.** Loans, affiliates, B teams and academy promotion are implemented and
  independently audited (`placement.rs`, `tools/roster_audit/oracle.py`).
* **Integrity checks and reporting.** `checks.rs` + `report.rs` make silent data loss visible;
  the reference report lists every generated player and staff member.
* **The season harness.** Multi-season, whole-world runs already measure transfers, finances and
  timing (`docs/APFM_MARKET_FINAL_VALIDATION.md`).
* **Distribution guard.** The licensing invariant is already tested, so D7B inherits a safety net.

## 5. D7B plan

Estimates are relative (S ≈ 1 week, M ≈ 2–3 weeks, L ≈ 4–6 weeks for one engineer), not
commitments. Each phase ends with a named check and a rollback point.

### Phase 0 — Scope and guardrails (S)

* Write the target scope: which nations/leagues, active vs dormant, free-agent policy, clauses in
  or out, continental in or out.
* Add the expanded-shape fixtures (synthetic), keep distribution guard green.
* **Exit:** signed-off scope document; no code.

### Phase 1 — Make the world shape configurable (M)

* Replace `EXPECTED_CLUBS` / `EXPECTED_COMPETITIONS` / `EXPECTED_CUPS` with values derived from
  the target set.
* Make `perimeter()` read the selected targets, not a hard-coded `playable/simulated` filter.
* Update `checks.rs`, `report.rs` and the journal to validate coverage and uniqueness.
* **Exit:** the current 12-league world builds byte-identically; a synthetic 2-league world builds
  and fails loudly on missing data.

### Phase 2 — Prove one new pyramid end-to-end (M/L)

* Pick one nation already partly modelled (e.g. England's League Two or Italy's Serie C) and
  complete its target row, rules, calendar and finance calibration.
* Run the adapter and 3–5 seasons; compare standings, promotion/relegation, finances, transfers.
* **Exit:** new division plays a full season; no regression in the existing 12 leagues; a written
  calibration note per division.

### Phase 3 — Dormant wide world (L)

* Import competitions outside the active scope as dormant definitions with membership and
  calendar.
* Test scoreline resolution, standings, cup progression, promotion/relegation and season rollover
  for dormant competitions.
* Benchmark day/season cost at 360 and 1,184 clubs.
* **Exit:** measured budget met; dormant coverage tests for each competition class; no user-visible
  regression in the active scope.

### Phase 4 — Data quality and pools at scale (M)

* Re-run the gap audit per new league (names, DOB, nation, wages, contract ends, club nation).
* Decide and implement the free-agent/unsattached model for the wider world (pool vs full).
* Validate staff floors and excluded-role policy.
* **Exit:** import report shows every fallback bounded and counted; no silent drops.

### Phase 5 — Persistence, payload and UI (M)

* Measure world JSON size, load time and memory at 360 / 1,184 clubs.
* Design lazy loading or projections where a screen cannot cope.
* Add save/load round-trip tests with a non-default expanded world.
* **Exit:** payload budget agreed and met; save migration proven; no `any`/raw CSS/string gaps.

### Phase 6 — Validation and release (M)

* 5-season deterministic runs on at least two seeds, whole active world.
* Licensing review; confirm no FM data in git/bundle.
* Documentation: discovery/readiness updates, import notes, known limitations.
* **Exit:** preflight + reviewers green; GO decision recorded per scope.

### 5.1 What is explicitly out of D7B

* Importing FM25/26/27 databases or editor files (none exist in this download).
* Bundling prebuilt worlds or FM-derived fixtures.
* Changing M12, the match engine, or gameplay balance outside the import/finance calibration.
* Claiming continental competitions without verified rules.
* Pushing or merging automatically.

## 6. Validation strategy

| Concern | Check |
|---|---|
| Shape | Expected counts derived from targets; clubs unique across competitions; every target has a name/nation/level |
| Determinism | Same source + config + seed ⇒ same world hash; source SHA-256 unchanged |
| Coverage | Every imported club/player/staff appears in the report or is journaled with a reason |
| Identity | Internal ids unique; `fm-p-*` maps to `world.db person.id`; shared FM UIDs handled |
| Scope | Active competitions fully simulated; dormant ones resolved by scoreline; no out-of-scope match leaks into the user's day |
| Persistence | Round-trip save/load with non-default values; fresh reader for write-back |
| Performance | Season harness budget at 240 / 360 / 1,184 clubs |
| Licensing | `distribution_guard.rs` green; no world in git or bundle |
| i18n | Any new player-visible string in every locale (D7B should add none to the importer) |

The current reference run (23/23 checks) is the baseline; every phase must keep it green.

## 7. Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Performance collapses at L2 | High | High | Dormant tier, benchmark gate, cap active pyramid |
| Reference data is wrong for a league | High | Medium | Provenance per rule, one-pyramid pilot, reviewer sign-off |
| Licensing breach | Low | Critical | Existing guard tests, synthetic fixtures only |
| Save format drift | Medium | High | Version detection, schema facts, explicit failure |
| Silent data loss | Medium | High | Coverage checks, journal codes, report counts |
| Scope creep (“just add all leagues”) | High | High | Tiered decision table, phase exits |
| Finance unrealism in lower divisions | High | Medium | Per-division calibration and 5-season runs |
| Payload/UI regression | Medium | Medium | Measure early in Phase 5, projections if needed |

## 8. Open questions for the product owner

1. Is the goal **more real players in the same 12 leagues**, or **more leagues**? They are
   different projects (B4 vs B2/B3).
2. Is a mostly **dormant** wide world acceptable, or must every imported league be fully
   simulated?
3. Which nations/leagues are the first expansion target, and in what order?
4. Are contract clauses, continental competitions and staff roles in scope, or documented as
   out?
5. What is the acceptable season-simulation time and world-file size on the target machine?
6. Should the free-agent pool scale with the world, stay at 3,000, or become a full population?
7. Is FM24 2026/27 the only supported source, or must D7B anticipate another save version?

---

## 9. Bottom line

* The **81,762-player figure is real** for the source, but only 13,157 are in the playable world.
* The source is a **third-party FM24 2026/27 save**, not an FM25/26/27 database and not an editor
  file.
* The shipped 240-club world is a deliberate, validated perimeter; the adapter is hard-wired to
  it.
* The architecture already has the two ingredients a wide world needs — **simulation scopes** and
  a **dormant scoreline tier** — but the data reference, competition rules, performance envelope
  and licensing constraints are not yet in place for L2/L3.
* **Recommendation:** approve **D7B Phase 0–2** (configurable shape + one new pyramid), re-measure,
  then decide on Phase 3. Do **not** attempt L3 (“all 44,032 clubs / 81,762 players”) as a
  simulated world.
